Telugu (తెలుగు, [t̪eluɡu]) is the most widely spoken Dravidian language. It is an official language of the Indian states of Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, and West Bengal, as well as the Yanam district of the union territory of Puducherry. Telugu-speaking diaspora communities also exist in North America, the Persian Gulf, Europe, and Australia. It is a protected language in South Africa and the fastest-growing language in the United States.

Telugu is designated as a classical language by the government of India. Telugu words began appearing in Prakrit inscriptions between the 2nd and 5th centuries CE, and a continuous literary tradition began in the 11th century. Ethnologue (2026) approximates 83 million L1 speakers of Telugu around the world, with a combined L1 and L2 total of 96 million speakers.

Writing System

Written Telugu utilizes an abugida script. Written from left to right syllabically, it has 16 vowels, 36 consonants, and three letters that function as both.

The modern Telugu script went into use circa 1300 CE. Its predecessors, from most recent to ancient, include the Telugu-Kannada, Bhattiprolu, and Brahmi scripts. The Telugu script is also used to write Koya, Konda, Gondi, and Lambadi.

Notable Telugu texts

Nannaya’s Andhra Mahabharatam, composed in the 11th century, is often considered the first Telugu literary composition, despite evidence of Telugu literature prior to that date. Andhra Bharatam is a Telugu version of the Mahabharata. Another notable text is the Andhrasabdacintamani, a medieval grammar that is best known as the earliest comprehensive grammar of the Telugu language. Other notable works include the Molla Ramayanam, a 16th-century version of the Ramayana written in layman’s Telugu by Atukuri Molla, and Kanyasulkam, a highly influential play written by Gurujada Apparao in 1892.
