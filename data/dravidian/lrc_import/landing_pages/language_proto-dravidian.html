Proto-Dravidian is the linguistic reconstruction of the common ancestor of the Dravidian languages. This hypothetical language was proposed by historical linguistics based on rigorous comparison of grammatical, lexical, phonetic, morphological, and syntactic features across all known Dravidian languages. As such, it is not attested in historical records, but linguists hypothesize that the language was first spoken between 4500 and 2500 BCE. The origin of the Dravidians and Proto-Dravidian remains unknown. More than a dozen Dravidian loanwords can be found in the Rigveda, and the presence of retroflex consonants in Sanskrit is considered the byproduct of contact between Sanskrit and speakers of Dravidian languages. The Urheimat, or region in which the proto-language is hypothesized to have been spoken, is generally considered to be somewhere on the Indian subcontinent.
