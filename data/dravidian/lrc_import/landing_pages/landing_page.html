Brief introduction to the Dravidian Languages

The South Asian subcontinent is home to four distinct language families. The Dravidian language family is the second largest, with 250 million speakers primarily in South India, northeast Sri Lanka, and southwest Pakistan. Sizeable diasporas reside outside of South Asia, notably in Australia, Canada, Fiji, Indonesia, Malaysia, Martinique, Mauritius, Myanmar, Singapore, South Africa, the United States, the United Kingdom, and Trinidad. The most commonly spoken Dravidian languages are Telugu, Tamil, Kannada, and Malayalam.

Proto-Dravidian is the linguistic reconstruction of the common ancestor of the modern Dravidian languages, which are grouped as South Dravidian, South-Central Dravidian, Central Dravidian, and North Dravidian in accordance with their distribution across the subcontinent.
