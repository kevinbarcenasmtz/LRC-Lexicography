Tamil (தமிழ், [t̪əˈmɪɻ]) is a Dravidian language. It is an official language of the Indian state of Tamil Nadu and the union territory of Puducherry, where it is most widely spoken. Tamil is also an official language in Sri Lanka and Singapore. Tamil-speaking diaspora communities, typically divided as the Indian Tamil diaspora and the Sri Lankan Tamil diaspora, also exist in other regions of Southeast Asia, North America, the Persian Gulf, Europe, and Australia.

Tamil was the first of the many languages in India to be recognized as a classical language by the central government in 2004. It is also considered one of the world’s oldest surviving classical languages, designated as such for its rich, independent, and enduring literary tradition. Ethnologue (2026) approximates 79 million L1 speakers of Tamil around the world, with a combined L1 and L2 total of 86 million speakers.

Writing System

Written Tamil utilizes an abugida script that evolved from the Brahmi script. Written from left to right syllabically, it has 12 vowels, 18 consonants, and one special character that is characterized as neither a vowel nor a consonant.

The earliest Tamil-Brahmi inscriptions, used to write Old Tamil, date back to the third century BCE. This script underwent significant change over time, splitting into the Vatteluttu and Pallava-Chola scripts, before eventually stabilizing in the 16th century CE with the introduction of the printing press. Tamil was the first Indian language to be printed.

Notable Tamil texts

The oldest extant grammar text is the Tolkāppiyam. It is estimated to have been composed circa 250 BCE and is the oldest surviving and most important text of Sangam literature. Sangam literature refers to early classical Tamil literature, typically dated between 100 BCE and 250 CE. Tolkāppiyam contains about 1,612 poems, divided into three categories, each with nine chapters: Ezhuthadhikaram (orthography), Solladhikaram (etymology), and Poruladhikaram (poetry, prosody, and figures of speech). Other notable classical texts include the Aimperumkāppiyankal, or the Five Great Epics. These were written between the 5th and 10th centuries and covered the religious and sociocultural dynamics of the Tamil people. Three of these five epics are written by Tamil Jains, while the other two are attributed to Tamil Buddhists.
