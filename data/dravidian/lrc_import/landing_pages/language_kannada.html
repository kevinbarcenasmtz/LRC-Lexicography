Kannada (ಕನ್ನಡ, [ˈkənːəɖa]) is a Dravidian language spoken primarily in the Indian state of Karnataka, with sizeable minorities in neighboring states. Kannada-speaking diaspora communities also exist in North America, the Persian Gulf, Europe, and Australia.

Kannada is designated as a classical language by the government of India. Kannada was the court language of numerous empires in South and Central India, and early Kannada literature was composed predominantly by Jain court poets. Ethnologue (2026) approximates 44 million L1 speakers of Kannada around the world, with a combined L1 and L2 total of 59 million speakers.

Writing System

Written Kannada utilizes an abugida script. Written from left to right syllabically, it has 13 vowels, 34 consonants, and two special characters.

The modern Kannada script went into use circa 1300 CE. Its predecessors, from most recent to ancient, include the Telugu-Kannada, Kadamba, and Brahmi scripts. The Kannada script is also used to write Tulu, Konkani, Kodava, Byari, and Sankethi.

Notable Kannada texts

Much of Kannada literature prior to the 18th century was religious. Beginning with Kavirajamarga (circa 850), the earliest known literary work in Kannada discussing poetics, and until the 12th century, Kannada literature was largely composed by the Jains. This was followed by the Lingayatism movement until the 15th century, after which Vaishnava literature thrived. Notable works across these periods include Vikramarjuna Vijaya, or the Pampa Bharata, and Basava Purana (translated and expanded from Telugu by Bhima Kavi in 1369 CE).
